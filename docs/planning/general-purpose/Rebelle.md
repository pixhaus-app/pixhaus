# Rebelle

## Quick facts
- Vendor / maintainer: Escape Motions (Slovak software company)
- License / pricing model: Perpetual license with annual update pass
- Price point (current): Standard Edition $89.99; Pro Edition $149.99 (both perpetual with 1 year of updates)
- Platforms: Windows, macOS
- First released: May 28, 2015 (full public release)
- Last meaningful update: Rebelle 8 released in Early Access July 2025, official release September 2025
- Source available: No. Proprietary.
- Primary use case: Natural media digital painting (not game sprite creation)

## Origin and purpose

Rebelle is specialized natural media painting software developed by Escape Motions, a Slovak software company. The application first released as public beta in April 2015 and reached full release on May 28, 2015. Rebelle focuses exclusively on simulating traditional painting media (oils, acrylics, watercolors, pastels, pencils, charcoal, ink) with photorealistic precision. The underlying simulation engine mimics paint pigment mixing, water diffusion, bristle behavior, and canvas texture. Rebelle is designed for digital painters seeking tactile, traditional-media-like workflows rather than for technical illustration or game development. The software is not relevant for sprite animation but is included in this research for context on natural media tools that artists sometimes use for character design and concept art that later becomes game assets.

## Drawing and painting tools

Rebelle includes an extensive collection of natural media brushes:
- **Wet media**: Oils (with multiple brushes for blending, impasto effects), acrylics, watercolors, ink pens.
- **Dry media**: Pastels (soft, oil, hard), colored pencils, charcoal (stick, pencil), graphite, markers, airbrush.

Each brush type is optimized for simulating the physical properties of its real-world counterpart. The Bristle Brush system (introduced in Rebelle 8) simulates individual brush strands with particle-based physics, creating organic, chaotic marks. The RealShader engine (also in v8) provides photorealistic rendering with soft shadows, impasto depth, and reflective light. Paint pigment color mixing simulates real pigment behavior; blending oils or watercolors on the canvas behaves like real paint (diffusion, opacity changes, pigment settling). Pressure sensitivity and tablet support are first-class.

## Pixel-specific features (or "How artists use it for sprite work")

Rebelle is a natural media painting tool and has no pixel-art features or sprite animation support. It is not designed for sprite creation or animation. Artists might use Rebelle for character design or concept art that later becomes sprite assets, but the tool itself is not suitable for pixel-art or frame-by-frame animation workflows. For sprite work, Rebelle is outside the scope of this research except as context for illustrators who transition concepts to game assets.

## Color and palette workflow

Rebelle uses a standard RGB color picker with hue/saturation/brightness sliders. The application includes a color mixer that simulates real pigment color mixing. When artists blend colors on the canvas, the resulting colors are computed based on pigment interaction rules, not simple blend modes. This creates authentic color behavior. Palette management is minimal; the focus is on simulating real paint mixing rather than managing swatches.

## Layer system

Rebelle supports multiple layers with opacity and blending. Layers can be organized for composition. However, the layer system is secondary to the natural media simulation; the core workflow emphasizes canvas interaction rather than layer manipulation.

## Animation features

Rebelle has no animation support. It is a painting tool, not an animation tool. No timeline, keyframes, frame-by-frame support, or sprite export. For game development, Rebelle output is used for character design or concept art, not for sprite animation.

## Export and import

Rebelle natively saves to the REB format (proprietary). Supports import of PNG, JPEG, TIFF, and PSD. Export options include PNG, JPEG, TIFF, and PDF.

For concept/design work:
- PNG export with transparency for import into game design tools or animation software.
- TIFF export for archival and professional printing.
- No sprite sheet or animation export.

## Scripting and extensibility

Rebelle does not support plugins or scripting. Workflows are extended via brush customization and layer management only.

## Engine integration

Rebelle is not a game engine. Output is used for character design and concept art in game development pipelines. Exports are imported into illustration software (Photoshop, Krita, Procreate) or design software (Affinity) for further refinement before becoming sprite assets.

## Workflow strengths

- Sophisticated natural media simulation; oils, watercolors, and pastels feel authentic.
- Photorealistic rendering with shadows, impasto, and reflective light.
- Pigment color mixing simulates real paint behavior.
- Pressure sensitivity and tablet support.
- One-time purchase with optional annual update pass (no subscription).
- Suitable for concept art and illustration-to-game-asset pipelines.

## Workflow gaps

- Not designed for sprite creation or animation.
- No pixel-art features.
- No animation timeline or frame-by-frame support.
- Specialized tool; not a general-purpose digital art application.
- Smaller community compared to general-purpose painting tools.
- Learning curve for natural media workflows; less intuitive than traditional brushes in Procreate or Krita.
- Performance can be demanding on older hardware due to simulation complexity.

## Notable uses

Rebelle is used by concept artists and illustrators creating character designs and environment artwork for games. Character concepts created in Rebelle are exported and refined in Photoshop or Krita before becoming sprite assets or 3D texture references. Not used for primary sprite creation or animation.

## Community and ecosystem

Smaller, specialized community focused on natural media painters. Official forums and Discord. YouTube tutorials on natural media simulation. Community shares brush presets and workflows. Integration with game development pipelines discussed in concept art communities rather than game development communities.

## Pricing details

Rebelle pricing as of v8 (September 2025):
- Standard Edition: $89.99 perpetual license (includes 1 year of updates).
- Pro Edition: $149.99 perpetual license (includes 1 year of updates and advanced features).
- Update Pass (annual): After 1 year, users can purchase an annual update pass ($price unknown, estimate $30-50) to continue receiving updates while retaining the perpetual license.
- Upgrade pricing: Users upgrading from Rebelle 7 receive 50% discount ($44.99 Standard, $74.99 Pro). Free upgrades for v7 owners who purchased on or after May 28, 2025.
- Free trial: Fully functional trial available; watermark on exports.

Rebelle follows a perpetual license model with optional annual updates, similar to classic software licensing before subscription became dominant. One-time purchase with indefinite license; updates are optional.
