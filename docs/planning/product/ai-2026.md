# AI in 2026 — what's possible now that wasn't six months ago

The original synthesis (`../synthesis/ai-opportunity.md`) bucketed gaps into "AI moves the needle / AI doesn't help / AI is the wrong answer." That framing is right but the bucketing was conservative. Several capabilities I dismissed have shipped in usable form. Several I called high-leverage have moved from research to productizable.

This doc reconsiders the surface honestly, given what SOTA actually delivers in May 2026, and informs the AI verb scope in `scope.md`.

## The model stack as it actually exists

This is the floor a Pixhaus AI feature can assume, not the ceiling.

**Vision-language models** — Claude Opus 4.6, Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5 class, Gemini 2 Pro — read sprite sheets natively, identify style, critique animations frame by frame, translate natural language into editor command sequences, and plan multi-step operations against a project. Tool-use is reliable. Multi-step agentic execution is reliable for tasks under 20-30 steps.

**Image generation** — SDXL and SD3 with the LoRA ecosystem; Flux for photorealism; community-fine-tuned pixel-art models (Retro Diffusion's licensed-asset training, PixelLab's dedicated model, Astropulse's Pixel Art XL LoRA). ControlNet for pose conditioning. IP-Adapter and reference-image conditioning for character consistency. Inpainting and outpainting at production quality. The "style consistency is the AI tool curse" line from the original synthesis is dated — character LoRAs trained in 30 minutes plus IP-Adapter solve the consistency problem most of the way.

**Video generation** — Sora, Veo 3, Runway Gen-4, Luma Dream Machine, Kling. Direct video-out is overkill for sprite animation, but the underlying frame-interpolation and motion-conditioned generation models are exactly the primitives Pixhaus needs for in-betweening and motion transfer.

**Pose and 3D awareness** — DensePose, MediaPipe Pose, SAPIENS for body extraction from video. Zero-1-to-3, ImageDream, Stable3D-class models for view synthesis from a single image. Single-image 3D reconstruction (TripoSR-class) is good enough to drive 4-direction or 8-direction sprite generation from one view.

**Audio** — beat detection at SOTA, music-driven timing extraction, audio-to-lip-sync (Wav2Lip, SadTalker descendants), sound effect generation (AudioGen, MusicGen).

**On-device** — quantized 7B-13B LLMs run on consumer GPUs and Apple Silicon. Stable Diffusion Turbo runs at 1-2 seconds per image on consumer hardware. WebGPU brings inference into the browser. The architecture choice "bring-your-own backend" looks better than ever — local-first AI is now a real option for users who don't want to send pixels to a third party.

## What the original synthesis was too conservative about

These are revisions to the original `ai-opportunity.md` analysis, not retractions.

### "Text-to-finished-sprite is the wrong UX"

Half right, half wrong. As the primary workflow it's still wrong — slot machine. As a starting-point workflow with full project context (active palette, character reference, animation history, style examples) it's a real entry point. The artist describes what they want, the AI proposes a layer, the artist refines or rejects. This is "generate-then-edit" rather than "generate-or-nothing."

The line that holds: never as auto-complete while drawing. The line that bends: as an explicit verb on a sprite that doesn't yet exist, with rich context, generation is a legitimate starting point.

### "Real-time AI suggestions interrupt flow"

Mostly still true, but with a caveat. Always-on autocomplete that pulls focus is bad. Ghost previews that materialize only on hotkey or hover, and only render in a sidebar or tooltip rather than over the canvas, are not bad. On-device inference makes the latency acceptable. The rule is "the artist invokes the AI" — and that invocation can be a passive hover instead of an explicit menu click.

### "Animation from a single still is auto-animation, which is bad"

Wrong as stated. A still + a motion reference (video clip, MOCAP, even text describing the motion) + a character reference is not slot-machine generation. It's pose-conditioned synthesis where the artist owns intent and the AI handles the in-betweens. The 2024 generation of pose-conditioned video models (AnimateAnyone, ChampAnyone, MotionDirector lineage) already shipped this; 2026 versions are production-ready.

### "Style consistency is the AI tool curse"

Dated. With per-project LoRA fine-tuning that runs in 15-30 minutes on a single consumer GPU, plus IP-Adapter for in-context reference, plus palette-conditioned decoding, the style consistency problem has moved from "unsolved" to "engineering work." Pixhaus can ship a one-click "learn this project's style" command that produces a per-project mini-model the rest of the AI verbs use as a baseline. Scenario built a business on this; the open-source equivalent is now feasible.

## Gaps from the synthesis, re-evaluated

The gaps doc (`../synthesis/gaps.md`) listed 13 open problems. Re-mapping each against 2026 capabilities:

| Gap | Status in 2026 |
|---|---|
| Pixel-perfect editing + skeletal animation don't coexist | Engineering problem; AI doesn't fix it. Pixhaus's answer is to skip skeletal entirely and reach for it via auto-mesh-deformation. |
| Cross-frame consistency for hand-drawn animation | **AI now solves a meaningful slice** via in-betweening (frame interpolation with palette respect). |
| Style consistency across batches | **Solved enough** by per-project LoRAs + IP-Adapter + reference conditioning. |
| Asset variations (palette swaps, equipment, expressions) | **Solved enough** by reference-conditioned generation with constraint masks. |
| Tile autotile generation is tedious | **Tractable** by VLM-driven autotile rule inference + image generation for in-between transitions. |
| Real-time game engine preview during sprite editing | Engineering problem; AI doesn't fix it. Pipe to a hot-reload importer. |
| Multi-angle / 8-direction character generation | **Tractable** via single-image view synthesis + style transfer. |
| Collaboration is non-existent | Engineering problem; CRDTs solve it, AI doesn't. Out of scope. |
| Frame-accurate animation preview is approximate | Engineering problem; render-loop synchronization, not AI. |
| Sprite asset QA is manual | **Newly solved** by VLM critique — read the sprite sheet, surface issues. |
| Animation reference matching is manual | **Newly solved** by pose extraction + motion transfer + frame timing alignment. |
| Multiplayer asset library workflow | Engineering problem; AI doesn't fix it. Out of scope. |
| Aseprite-specific gaps (group blend modes, etc.) | Engineering problems. |

Six of thirteen gaps are now AI-tractable, up from the four implied in the original synthesis. That's "most problems have been solved already" landing concretely.

## How this maps to the verb scope

The 14 verbs in `scope.md` (S23-S36 in `../work/streams.md`) span the full set of currently-tractable capabilities:

- **Inbetween, Continue, Extend, Variant, Cleanup, Tile, Critique** — the well-understood verbs that map to the high-leverage gaps above.
- **Project style learning** — the one-time per-project LoRA training that makes every other verb stronger.
- **Conversational editing** — the agentic NL-to-commands surface, enabled by tool-use-capable VLMs.
- **Motion-from-video** — pose-conditioned timing extraction.
- **Auto-mesh-deformation** — the no-bones path to Live2D-class results.
- **Audio-driven timing** — beat detection and lip sync.
- **Tileset-from-description** — the terminal version of Tile.
- **Sketch finishing** — pose+silhouette to finished sprites.

All of them are within reach of the 2026 model stack. None of them are research-grade. The implementation work is engineering against documented APIs and well-understood model behavior.

## The architectural implication

The verbs aren't the product. The verb-plugin protocol is the product. Every capability above — and every capability that ships in the next 18 months that nobody has imagined yet — should arrive as a plugin, not a fork of the editor.

A well-designed verb plugin protocol describes:

1. **Inputs** — which layers, frames, regions, palettes, references the verb consumes.
2. **Outputs** — what new layers / frames / metadata it produces, all non-destructive.
3. **Inference backend** — local, hosted, BYO-API-key, or chained workflow (e.g., a ComfyUI graph). The protocol shouldn't care; the verb declares what it needs and the runtime resolves it.
4. **Preview model** — every verb produces a previewable result before the artist commits. The accept / refine / reject flow is universal.
5. **Cost and latency contract** — the verb declares "this takes 30 seconds and costs $0.05" or "this runs locally in 5 seconds" so the UI can render appropriate progress and warnings.

If this protocol is right, Pixhaus becomes the platform for AI-native pixel art and other people add the verbs we didn't predict. If it's wrong, every new model release demands a new release of the editor.

This is the most important AI-related decision in the project. It's bedrock spec B5 in `../work/bedrock.md` and the work is dispatched to a single agent before any verb stream starts. Get the shape right.

## The verbs that exist later, that nobody has yet asked for

The work organization deliberately leaves room for verbs that the model stack of 2026.5 or 2027 enables and that we haven't named. Reference-driven generation with proper attribution, real-time collaborative AI that takes turns with the artist, scene-level composition that places sprites into procedurally-generated levels — these are likely candidates. The protocol should accept all of them as plugins without editor changes. That's what makes the verb count irrelevant. The verb count today is 14. The verb count next year might be 40. None of that should require a Pixhaus release.
